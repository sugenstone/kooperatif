# 08 --- Investments, Assets, Real Estate and Business Holdings

## Purpose

Model cooperative investments and assets without confusing expected
return, profit, valuation and cash.

## Investment

The cooperative may invest in an activity/project and expect income each
Period or on another schedule.

Candidate Investment fields: - name; - type; - start/end dates; -
invested principal/cost; - funding Financial Account(s); - expected
return schedule; - actual recognized income; - actual cash received; -
status; - documents; - responsible parties; - Policy/Decision
references.

## Expected income

Expected income is forecast/planning data.

For each expected Period/date: - expected amount/value; - expected
date/Period; - assumptions; - status.

When actual income occurs, record actual event separately.

Do not overwrite expected amount with actual amount.

## Actual investment result

Separate: - realized income; - cash received; - receivable; -
expense/cost; - valuation change; - unrealized gain/loss.

## Real Estate owned by cooperative

A Property may include: - identity/name; - address/location
description; - acquisition date; - acquisition cost; - ownership
share; - legal/document references; - current status; - valuation
history; - rental contracts; - income/expense history; - disposal
history.

## Cooperative as landlord

When cooperative rents out owned Property: - Lease Contract; - tenant
Party; - term; - rent schedule; - deposit if modeled; - rent
Receivables; - actual receipts; - arrears; - expenses; - contract
documents.

Scheduled rent is not automatically cash received.

## Cooperative as tenant

The cooperative may rent Property from another owner.

Model: - Lease Contract; - landlord Party; - term; - rent schedule; -
deposit if modeled; - rent Payables/Expenses; - actual Payments from
Financial Accounts; - contract documents.

Do not create an owned Property Asset merely because cooperative rents
it.

## Property valuation

Valuations are historical records: - valuation date; - amount; -
method; - valuer/source; - document; - confidence/status if useful.

A new valuation does not rewrite acquisition cost.

Valuation gain is not automatically realized profit/cash.

## Property sale/disposal

Disposal should distinguish: - sale contract/value; - receivable; - cash
receipt; - asset derecognition; - realized result according to approved
accounting rule.

Exact realized-gain formula is unresolved.

## Business Holding

The cooperative may own a share/interest in an operating business.

Candidate fields: - business Party/entity; - ownership
percentage/units; - acquisition date; - acquisition cost; - capital
contributions; - current status; - valuation history; -
agreements/documents; - Decision reference.

## Business result vs cooperative income

The business may earn profit without cash reaching cooperative.

Separate: 1. business-reported profit/information; 2. declared
distribution/dividend/right; 3. cooperative Receivable; 4. cash received
into Financial Account.

Do not treat investee profit as cooperative cash automatically.

## Additional capital

Further investment into a Business Holding: - creates new
investment/cost basis event; - reduces Financial Account; - does not
automatically become Expense.

## Sale/transfer of holding

Preserve: - disposed percentage/units; - counterparty; - date; - agreed
consideration; - receivable/cash; - remaining holding; - realized result
when formula approved.

## Net asset/value reporting

Assets may contribute to cooperative Net Asset Value only under a
defined Policy.

Do not invent valuation inclusion rules.

## Decision/approval

Investment, Property acquisition/disposal and Business Holding actions
may require Board/Approval rules.

Thresholds are configurable.

## Reporting

Candidate reports: - investment portfolio; - expected vs actual
returns; - Property portfolio; - rent receivables/payables; - valuation
history; - Business Holdings; - cash invested vs cash returned; -
realized/unrealized results.

## Open decisions
